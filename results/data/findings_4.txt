

1. Directed search finds failures that uniform sampling does not.
   Simulations to the first counterexample (mean over encounter types):
     random search        34.0
     CMA-ES               136
     Bayesian opt.        40
   Worst robustness reached: random -134 m, CMA-ES -371 m, BO -578 m.
   A test campaign that samples is measuring frequency, not safety.

2. Counting violations overstates what a falsifier found.
   Distinct counterexamples after clustering: CMA-ES 18 from 28 violations; BO 43 from 82.
   CMA-ES concentrates on one basin; BO spreads. Neither is strictly better,
   and the difference only shows up if diversity is measured.

3. Behaviour cloning loses safety that imitation error does not predict.
   Held-out imitation MSE was 0.00137 (small probe set -- policy was loaded from checkpoint, not retrained), yet closed-loop safety fell from 0.994 (MPC) to 0.472 (policy),
   and 5th-percentile separation from 953 m to 95 m.
   The policy's own errors carry it into states the expert never visited.

4. Learned controllers are flattered by random testing.
   Learned policy (BC)    random ρ =     -709 m, CMA-ES ρ =     -772 m (gap 63 m)
   Rule-based COLREGS     random ρ =     -441 m, CMA-ES ρ =     -275 m (gap -166 m)

5. Distillation makes the expert deployable.
   MPC 36689 µs per decision vs 36.9 µs for the policy (995x),
   at a model size of 40 kB.
   The safety cost of that speed is item 3, and it is not small.


  Falsification attacks the system under test, not the simulator. Every
  counterexample here is a reproducible scenario specification, replayable
  from its parameter vector alone.
