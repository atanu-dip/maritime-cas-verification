
1. Ensemble disagreement separates reducible from irreducible error.
   Median epistemic share of range variance: 0.26.
   The part that shrinks with better models is distinguishable from the part
   that does not, which is what tells a designer where to spend effort.

2. The raw uncertainty estimate is not well calibrated.
   Standardised residuals: mean 4.18, sd 26.43 (should be 0 and 1). ECE = 0.138.
   DCPA is a distance and cannot be symmetric near a close approach, so a
   Gaussian summary of it is wrong in a predictable direction.

3. Conformal prediction restores a guarantee without distributional
   assumptions.
   At alpha = 0.10 the empirical coverage is 0.890 against a 0.900 target,
   at a mean cost of 4225 m of conservatism.

4. Uncertainty awareness must include knowing when the uncertainty is
   meaningless.
   Using the conformal bound before the track converges made the CAS LESS
   safe: sigma_DCPA is then so large that the bound is below the limit almost
   always, so the ship manoeuvres continuously on noise. Gating on track
   readiness was required before the bound helped at all.
   Point estimate: safety 0.650, progress loss 3.41%.
   Risk-aware (Risk-aware (α=0.2)): safety 0.633, progress loss 3.31%, 5th-pct separation 216 m vs 174 m.

5. COLREGS role inversion is real, and strongly asymmetric.
   Dangerous inversions (give-way -> stand-on): 0.0339 of steps.
   Conservative inversions (stand-on -> give-way): 0.1911 of steps.
   The asymmetry has a cause: most targets are power-driven, so almost any
   misclassification *raises* the target's apparent privilege and pushes
   own-ship toward manoeuvring. Dangerous inversions need the rarer case of a
   genuinely privileged vessel read as ordinary -- rarer, and worse.
